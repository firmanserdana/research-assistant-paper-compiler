## EMG Decoding on Spinal Cord Injured Patients (1 papers)

### Sensing and decoding the neural drive to paralyzed muscles during attempted movements of a person with tetraplegia using a sleeve array
**Authors:** Jordyn E. Ting; Alessandro Del Vecchio; Devapratim Sarma; Nikhil Verma; Samuel C. Colachis; Nicholas V. Annetta; Jennifer L. Collinger; Dario Farina; Douglas J. Weber
**DOI:** 10.1152/jn.00220.2021
**TRL:** 4
**Keywords:** neural drive, EMG decomposition, spinal cord injury, motor unit, wearable sensors, motor control
**Summary:** This paper demonstrates that **wearable electrode arrays combined with machine learning can noninvasively record and decode motor unit firing rates in paralyzed muscles of individuals with motor complete spinal cord injury, even when no visible movement occurs**. The key innovation is the extraction of task-specific neural drive signals from residual motor unit activity using EMG decomposition, enabling accurate classification of movement intent and simulated real-time control of virtual limbs. This approach has significant potential for **biorobotics**, as it provides a practical, noninvasive interface for controlling assistive devices based on volitional motor commands in severely paralyzed patients.

## Stimulating Peripheral Nerve to Enable Hand And/or Finger Control (1 papers)

### Soft robotics and functional electrical stimulation advances for restoring hand function in people with SCI: a narrative review, clinical guidelines and future directions
**Authors:** Lucas R. L. Cardoso; Vanesa Bochkezanian; Arturo Forner-Cordero; Alejandro Melendez-Calderon; Antonio P. L. Bo
**DOI:** 10.1186/s12984-022-01043-1
**TRL:** 3
**Keywords:** soft robotics, functional electrical stimulation, hand rehabilitation, wearable devices
**Summary:** The paper introduces an innovative approach that integrates **soft robotics** with **functional electrical stimulation (FES)** to advance hand rehabilitation, focusing on wearable devices that provide adaptive, safe, and personalized assistance for patients with impaired hand function. This combined methodology leverages the compliance and comfort of soft robotic actuators with the neuromuscular activation offered by FES, potentially enabling more effective, task-specific rehabilitation and expanding the capabilities of biorobotics for neurorehabilitation and assistive technologies. The synergy of these technologies represents a significant step toward intelligent, user-friendly rehabilitation systems that can be deployed in clinical and home settings, with broad implications for future biorobotics research and application.
