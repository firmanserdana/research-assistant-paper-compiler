## EMG Decoding on Spinal Cord Injured Patients (1 papers)

### Neurorehabilitation Through Synergistic Man-Machine Interfaces Promoting Dormant Neuroplasticity in Spinal Cord Injury: Protocol for a Nonrandomized Controlled Trial
**Authors:** Alkinoos Athanasiou; Konstantinos Mitsopoulos; Apostolos Praftsiotis; Alexander Astaras; Panagiotis Antoniou; Niki Pandria; Vasileia Petronikolou; Konstantinos Kasimis; George Lyssas; Nikos Terzopoulos; Vasilki Fiska; Panagiotis Kartsidis; Theodoros Savvidis; Athanasios Arvanitidis; Konstantinos Chasapis; Alexandros Moraitopoulos; Kostas Nizamis; Anestis Kalfas; Paris Iakovidis; Thomas Apostolou; Ioannis Magras; Panagiotis Bamidis
**DOI:** 10.2196/41152
**TRL:** 3
**Keywords:** man-machine interface, EMG decoding, spinal cord injury, neurorehabilitation, wearable robotics
**Summary:** The key innovation of this study is the development and clinical testing of a **synergistic neurorehabilitation protocol** for spinal cord injury using multiple immersive **man-machine interfaces**—including EMG-decoding wearable robotics, brain-computer interface (BCI)-controlled robotic arms, and augmented reality modules—to promote dormant neuroplasticity and optimize motor recovery. By integrating rich multimodal feedback (visual, tactile, and kinesthetic) and serious gaming, the protocol aims to retrain central nervous system circuits and enhance motivation, with potential impact for biorobotics research in designing individualized assistive technologies and advancing understanding of neuroplasticity mechanisms in SCI rehabilitation.

## Intracortical Decoding on Hand and Finger Kinematics (1 papers)

### Convolutional neural networks decode finger movements in motor sequence learning from MEG data
**Authors:** Aleksey Zabolotniy; Russell Weili Chan; Victoria Moiseeva; Tommaso Fedele
**DOI:** 10.3389/fnins.2025.1623380
**TRL:** 4
**Keywords:** finger movement decoding, MEG, convolutional neural networks, motor learning
**Summary:** The key innovation of this paper is the validation of a compact **convolutional neural network (LF-CNN)** for fast and reliable decoding of individual finger movements from non-invasive **magnetoencephalographic (MEG)** recordings, achieving 80–85% accuracy for single-finger discrimination and over 95% for hand laterality. LF-CNN offers significant advantages in computational efficiency and interpretability over more complex deep learning architectures, enabling direct examination of neurophysiological patterns in the motor cortex. This approach holds substantial promise for **biorobotics**, as it enables high-resolution, interpretable decoding of fine motor actions from non-invasive signals, facilitating advanced brain–computer interfaces for robotic control and neuroprosthetics.

## Stimulating Peripheral Nerve to Enable Hand And/or Finger Control (1 papers)

### Soft robotics and functional electrical stimulation advances for restoring hand function in people with SCI: a narrative review, clinical guidelines and future directions
**Authors:** Lucas R. L. Cardoso; Vanesa Bochkezanian; Arturo Forner-Cordero; Alejandro Melendez-Calderon; Antonio P. L. Bo
**DOI:** 10.1186/s12984-022-01043-1
**TRL:** 3
**Keywords:** Functional electrical stimulation, soft robotics, hand rehabilitation, neuroprosthesis, spinal cord injury
**Summary:** The paper reviews recent advances in **soft robotics (SR) and functional electrical stimulation (FES) wearable devices** for hand function recovery in spinal cord injury, highlighting their technical features and functional outcomes. The key innovation is the identification of complementary roles for SR and FES—SR offers adaptable, comfortable, and portable assistance, while FES enhances neuroplasticity—suggesting that hybrid systems could maximize rehabilitation efficacy and user independence. This synthesis provides a roadmap for biorobotics research, emphasizing the need for improved user intent detection, portability, and standardized outcome measures to facilitate clinical translation.
