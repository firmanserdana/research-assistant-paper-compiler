## EMG Decoding on Spinal Cord Injured Patients (1 papers)

### Sensing and decoding the neural drive to paralyzed muscles during attempted movements of a person with tetraplegia using a sleeve array
**Authors:** Jordyn E. Ting; Alessandro Del Vecchio; Devapratim Sarma; Nikhil Verma; Samuel C. Colachis; Nicholas V. Annetta; Jennifer L. Collinger; Dario Farina; Douglas J. Weber
**DOI:** 10.1152/jn.00220.2021
**TRL:** 3
**Keywords:** High‑density EMG, neural drive decoding
**Summary:** The paper demonstrates that **high‑density, wearable EMG (a forearm “sleeve” array) combined with motor‑unit decomposition and machine‑learning decoding** can sense task‑specific neural drive to hand muscles in a person with clinically complete tetraplegia, even when no overt movement is produced. By reliably classifying attempted single‑digit movements from residual motor‑unit firing patterns below the spinal lesion, it establishes a practical, noninvasive control interface that could be directly leveraged in biorobotics to drive multi‑DOF prosthetic hands, exoskeletons, or functional electrical stimulation systems from *movement intent* rather than residual kinematics.

## Intracortical Decoding on Hand and Finger Kinematics (1 papers)

### Decoding Handwriting Trajectories from Intracortical Brain Signals for Brain‐to‐Text Communication
**Authors:** Guangxiang Xu; Zebin Wang; Kedi Xu; Junming Zhu; Jianmin Zhang; Yueming Wang; Yaoyao Hao
**DOI:** 10.1002/advs.202505492
**TRL:** 4
**Keywords:** intracortical signals, handwriting kinematics, trajectory decoding, LSTM decoder, Utah array, motor cortex, brain-to-text, continuous trajectory reconstruction
**Summary:** This paper introduces an innovative decoding framework using an **LSTM-based model** with shape and temporal distortion compensation to reconstruct highly accurate, human-recognizable handwriting trajectories (correlation coefficient >0.75) from intracortical signals recorded via Utah arrays in the motor cortex of a paralyzed patient during attempted handwriting of complex characters. The framework resolves neural-movement misalignment via dynamic time warping for brain-to-text translation, achieving up to **91.1% recognition rate** on a 1000-character database and enabling multi-day data fusion for sustained performance across languages. For **biorobotics research**, it advances neural prosthetics by demonstrating multidimensional handwriting encoding (including 3D velocity, grip strength, and EMG), paving the way for universal, high-fidelity brain-controlled robotic writing interfaces.
